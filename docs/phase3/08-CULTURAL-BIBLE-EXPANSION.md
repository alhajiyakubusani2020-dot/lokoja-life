## PART I — WHAT MAKES LOKOJA FEEL LIKE LOKOJA (the identity grammar)

1. **The confluence IS the identity.** No single group owns the city — Bassa-Nge, Oworo, Nupe are the
   documented indigenous core, joined by Hausa, Ebira, Igala, Igbo, Edo, Tiv, Okun-Yoruba. NPC casting
   must reflect plurality; "Na we meet point" is the city's own framing (HIGH confidence).
2. **Belonging = generations, not ethnicity.** "My grandfather sell fish for Kabawa — we be natives here,
   no be settlers." Fifth-to-seventh-generation families are *natives* regardless of origin group. This
   is the town's core identity rule and becomes dialogue law.
3. **Language layering (MED, sourced):** Hausa = street/market lingua franca ("everyone can
   communicate in Hausa"); ethnic tongue at home; **Pidgin = the playful cross-ethnic bridge**; English
   formal. Hausa-named old quarters (Kabawa, Makera, Ungwan-Pawa) anchor the map.
4. **The name is a live argument — ship it as content.** Four etymologies, each gets a voice: Oworo
   "Ilu Oke Oja" · Hausa "Loko Ja" · Nupe "Patti Lukongi" · the official loko-tree story (red sap;
   Lokongoma = "tenth loko"). NPCs argue; the UI never rules (A-canon; Maiyaki's own rejection of the
   Nupe version is dialogue too).
5. **History flexes, phrased safely:** capital of the British Northern Protectorate (A10 phrasing —
   never "first capital of Nigeria" in encyclopaedia copy; NPCs may boast it and be argued with).
   Flora Shaw coined "Nigeria" from Patti (1897, Times essay). Baikie's model town: **1857–1860
   documented range (A4)** — NPCs may disagree about the year.
6. **Respect machinery:** prostrate/kneel greetings to elders; Maigari is THE central stool with
   sub-stools (Onu Igala Lokoja, Odeyani Anegbira, Olu of Oworo, Ohinoyi for Ebira) — never invent
   titles; never let an NPC skip a stool's style.
