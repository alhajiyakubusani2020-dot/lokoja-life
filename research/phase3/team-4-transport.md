## 1. Executive Findings (≤30 bullets, each ends [src N])

1. Lokoja's whole economy of movement hangs on ONE highway node: the Abuja–Lokoja (A2 corridor) dual carriageway through Koton-Karfe–Felele–Nataco is the country's critical north–south gateway and is simultaneously the busiest and most gridlock-prone road in the city's life [src 1, 7, 8].
2. The Abuja–Lokoja highway is UNDER CONSTRUCTION through 2025–2026 (dualisation; "completed sections" language from the Works Ministry, Dec 2025); Umahi terminated four Kogi road contracts for poor performance in 2024, and the Presidency was still publicly flogging contractors in Jan 2026 [src 6, 7].
3. Documented truck curfew: since July 2025 Kogi State restricts trailers/tankers/articulated vehicles to **11:00 p.m.–5:00 a.m.** daily — enforcement is weak (a fatal truck crash happened at Felele in daylight-violating hours, Aug 2025). The House of Representatives had asked for 10 p.m.–6 a.m. in Feb 2025 [src 3, 2].
4. Felele is the city's death corridor: "notorious spot for road crashes" at Felele bus-stop; a Federal University Lokoja shuttle bus was rammed by a wood truck on 17 Feb 2025; 10+ accidents on the Lokoja–Nataco–Felele stretch in 2025 alone; Reps demand a flyover + bypass + pedestrian bridges on the "high-risk one-kilometre stretch" [src 2, 3].
5. The FRSC Kogi sector command is an on-the-ground traffic authority in Lokoja — named sector commanders: Kumar Tsukwam (Aug 2025) and Lawan Lalu-Fagge (May 2026, a later rotation) [src 1, 4].
6. **Lokoja International Market is the city's biggest intra-town traffic bottleneck** — Kogi State + FRSC ran a joint sensitisation (May 2026) against roadside offloading, encroaching traders, indiscriminate keke parking, and passenger pickup outside approved loading points; a state traffic task force was being stood up after a 15 April 2026 Government House stakeholders' meeting [src 4].
7. **Kogi has NO functional mass-transit bus system as of March 2026** — an Lokoja-based NGO (INGRA) was still begging for state buses and "regulated transport routes and fare structures"; buses in Lokoja life are intercity coaches, not city buses [src 12].
8. NBS (Feb 2026, national): okada average fare **₦920.95** (+53% y/y), intra-city bus **₦1,195.75**, intercity bus per drop **₦8,108.81**, water transport **₦2,097.30** — NIGERIA-GENERIC anchors; Kogi-specific city-fare tables remain unverifiable from news [src 21].
9. Petrol reality for refuel maths (21 Oct 2026): landing cost ₦1,283.81/L (MEMAN), Dangote gantry ₦1,350/L, ex-depot ₦1,348–1,358, **Abuja-area pumps ₦1,370–₦1,429/L**; a proposed NNPCL ₦1,350 discount (8 Oct 2026) was NOT yet implemented — Lokoja sits on this Abuja-side price corridor [src 16].
10. CNG is the state's keke policy bet: Kogi received its first batch of **50 federal CNG tricycles** (Sep 2025; 19 went to the tricycle/okada union NACTOMORAS), but by Jun 2026 there was still only **one CNG station** serving the area, with long queues — a perfect scarcity-gameplay seam [src 9, 10].
11. The tricycle/okada union in Kogi is **NACTOMORAS** (National Association of Tricycle Owners, Motorcycle Owners and Riders Association), Kogi chairman Hon. Amos Onuche Isah; state transport commissioner is Atuluku Victor Levi. NURTW dominates parks in Lagos/southwest headlines but no NURTW-Lokoja park story surfaced in 2024–2026 searches [src 9].
12. The classic Lokoja intercity motor park on the Abuja axis is **Nataco/Natako Park** (spelling varies by paper): a driver who waited out the Aug 2025 gridlock "is here at Natako Park this Monday morning, trying to get passengers to return to Abuja" [src 1].
13. Crisis fares are documented, extreme, and dated: during the Sept 2026 gridlock an okada from Jamata to Nataco cost **₦6,000**, and people were charged up to **₦6,000 per person** just to cross the River Niger Bridge area — vs NBS national okada average ₦920.95 six months earlier [src 5, 21].
14. Flood days create a canoe economy: in the 2022 flood, New Market → Ganaja village by canoe cost **₦400–500/trip (up from ₦200)**, and crossing the Old Market to Kabawa cost ₦200/person+goods; Ganaja road — the ONLY road linking the North to Anambra/Benue/Edo — submerges at the 500 Housing Estate → Ganaja roundabout → extreme-Ganaja stretch [src 15, 14].
15. Felele–Ganaja geometry matches the game map: Ganaja Junction is documented as both a labour market ("where men, women compete for menial jobs", 2013) and a vanished-under-flood road node; a Ganaja flyover project existed under Gov Wada in 2016 (completion unverified) [src 25, 14, 24 — headline-only for 25/24].
16. Seasonal gridlock peaks are real and dated: Yuletide Dec 2025 (truck standoff, thousands stranded, contractors vanished for Christmas) and Easter Apr 2026 gridlocks both hit the Lokoja–Abuja axis — "ember months" rhetoric is standard in FRSC/monarch appeals [src 6, 3, 31 — headline-only for the Easter item].
17. Motor parks are security infrastructure: in Feb 2026 Kogi shut markets AND motor parks in 7 LGAs to starve bandits — including **Oshokoshoko Market and Motor Park** plus Jakura/Ogbagbon/Agbaja/Obajana/Abugi etc. in Lokoja LGA [src 13].
18. Fuel retail is being remade mid-2026: NNPC is converting to 50–70 "smart, self-service" energy hubs (petrol+CNG+LPG+EV, digital night dispensing, attendants retained) — a near-future look for the game's filling stations [src 17].
19. Okada bans are a Nigerian state-by-state patchwork (Lagos enforcement push Sep 2025; Kano reimposed a ban Dec 2025 over rider numbers) — Lokoja has NO okada ban story in any 2024–2026 search; okada remains Lokoja's dominant flexible mode [src 31 — headline-only].
20. The real Lokoja CBD street names already in the game's OSM binary read like a transport history: Lugard Road, Marine Road, Ahmadu Bello Way, IBB Way, Hassan Usman Katsina Road, Wada Road, Taiwo Road, Lokoja-Otukpo Road, Aliyu Attah Road, Chief Olusegun Obasanjo Way [repo 3].
21. Koton-Karfe (Egbura Igu), HQ of Kogi LGA, is the Abuja-side bridgehead of the Murtala Mohammed Bridge crossing of the Niger; FRSC describes the worst gridlock stretch as "Ogbobo/Ozi to Murtala Mohammed Bridge at Koton-Karfe" [src 19, 1].
22. The Murtala Mohammed Bridge (Koton-Karfe bridge over the Niger) and the Jamata/Nataco/Gegu-Eba/Banda/Gegu-Beki place-chain are the recurring gridlock landmarks of 2025–2026 reporting [src 1, 5].
23. Ferry/water transport has a deep Lokoja lineage: FG launched a water transport scheme in Lokoja (Jan 2013), a Lokoja–Onitsha ferry ran and then "hit the rocks" (2013), and Gov Ododo reaffirmed an NIWA water-transport partnership (Jul 2026) — but no scheduled 2025–2026 Lokoja ferry service could be verified [src 26, 27, 31 — headline-only].
24. NBS waterways fares rose 31.66% y/y by Feb 2026 (₦2,097.30) — canoe/boat pricing in the game should float with the flood mechanic, not be flat [src 21].
25. Nigeria's driving culture signals (danfo conductors, "go-slow", horn language) are LAGOS/NIGERIA-GENERIC, not Lokoja-verified: no source describing Lokoja horn codes or conductor calls surfaced; Lagos danfo even moved to digital fares (Aug 2026) [src 31 — headline-only; see Open Questions].
26. The game's own tuning (keke ≈₦300 for a ~3 km median trip, taxi ≈₦1,000) sits plausibly between the 2022-era ₦200-ish keke baseline and NBS 2026 national averages, but is presented as tuned, not sourced [repo 2; src 15, 21].
27. Kogi students are a traffic-politics force: FUL students threatened a highway blockade over truck crashes (Feb 2025) — traffic pain on the Felele corridor regularly turns into protest [src 31 — headline-only; corroborated by src 2].
28. Abaji–Koton-Karfe dualisation has been a rolling federal project since at least 2018 (FG threatened termination in May 2018) — the "endless roadworks" texture of the corridor is historical, not new [src 29 — headline-only].
29. Gridlock geography for simulation: congestion seeds at Gegu-Eba (Kogi LGA) and runs through Nataco (Lokoja LGA) and Gegu-Beki; Banda is <5 km from Lokoja town yet took 7+ hours during the Sept 2026 jam [src 5].
30. The game already simulates 'go-slow' as a travel event and a keke refuel stop; the evidence base now gives those events real-world texture (Felele crawl, market-day parking chaos, CNG queues) to hang mechanics on [repo 5; src 3, 4, 10].

## 4. Contradictions & Data Conflicts

1. **Truck curfew hours**: House of Representatives motion (Feb 2025) urged **10:00 p.m.–6:00 a.m.** within Lokoja metropolis [src 2]; the actual Kogi directive (July 2025) set **11:00 p.m.–5:00 a.m.** [src 3]. Record BOTH; use the state directive (11pm–5am) as canon, with the Reps version as political context.
2. **FRSC Kogi sector commander identity**: Kumar Tsukwam (Punch/NAN, Aug 2025) [src 1] vs Lawan Lalu-Fagge (Leadership, May 2026) [src 4]. Not a fact-conflict — a command rotation; both recorded, both usable as period-accurate NPC names.
3. **Nataco vs Natako spelling**: Punch/NAN prints "Natako Park"; Leadership prints "Nataco"; the Reps motion prints "Nataco". Same place, divergent romanisation; the game should pick ONE (suggest "Nataco") and note the variant.
4. **A2 naming**: Wikipedia's A2 article does not name Lokoja (says Abuja is linked by the A234) [src 18], while Nigerian press consistently calls the corridor "Abuja-Lokoja(-Okene-Benin)" road [src 8, 2]. Both kept: use "the A2 corridor / Abuja–Lokoja highway" in prose, avoid claiming a signed route number inside Lokoja city.
5. **Travel time Abuja→Lokoja**: "roughly two hours" (naijaproperty, Aug 2026, LOW) [src 33] vs 9 hours in the Aug 2025 gridlock [src 1] vs 7 h Banda→Lokoja (Sep 2026) [src 5]. Normal-vs-crisis split, both retained as calibration anchors (2× multiplier basis for jam days).
6. **Crisis "bridge crossing fee" ₦6,000/person (Sep 2026)** [src 5] vs NBS okada average ₦920.95 (Feb 2026) [src 21]. Not a contradiction but a 6–20× crisis surge; the mechanic of WHO charges (okada? boat? bribes?) is ambiguous → Open Question.
7. **CNG promise vs fuelling reality**: 50 CNG tricycles delivered (Sep 2025) [src 9] vs only ONE CNG station serving the area (Jun 2026) [src 10]. Both true; the gap IS the gameplay.
8. **Wikipedia "Lokoja founded 1857 by Baikie"** [src 20] vs the city's own older Nupe/Oworo settlement narrative in the same article — internal tension within one source; flag only if history teams rely on it.

### P0 (Phase 3 core, evidence-backed)
1. **Truck curfew clock (11 p.m.–5 a.m.)** — a real, datable Kogi law [src 3]. Implement: 23:00–05:00 removes articulated trucks from in-city Actor traffic; Felele/A2 artery keeps highway trucks only outside city bounds. Cheap, recognizable, citable.
2. **Rush-hour multipliers via existing `minutesMult`** — `calculateTravel` already accepts `minutesMult` (travel.ts:62). Slot multipliers: morning peak 1.4×, evening 1.3×, market-day +0.2×; grounded in market-day congestion + festive peaks (Dec 2025, Apr 2026) [src 4, 6, 31]. Keep `slotsPerTrip=1`.

Implement as a weather/event-scaled `fuelMult`-style multiplier for PERSONAL rides (M8 law in travel.ts:51-53 already distinguishes personal vs public), public fares "never surge" per city-rate fiction.

7. **Refuel pricing anchor** — pump ₦1,370–1,429/L (Oct 2026) with Dangote-era volatility [src 16]; `own-*` fuel-per-km rates (config.ts:160-163) should index to a `fuelPrice` constant, and scarcity days spike `fuelMult` (travel.ts M8).
